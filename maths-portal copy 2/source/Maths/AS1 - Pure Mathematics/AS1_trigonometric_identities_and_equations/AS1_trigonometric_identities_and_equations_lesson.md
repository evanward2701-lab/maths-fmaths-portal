## 4. Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

1. Derive and recall exact values for \(30^\circ,45^\circ,60^\circ\).
2. Use the unit circle to explain the signs of \(\sin\theta\), \(\cos\theta\) and \(\tan\theta\) in each quadrant.
3. Use graph symmetry and periodicity laws such as
   \[
   \sin x=\sin(180^\circ-x),\qquad
   \cos x=\cos(360^\circ-x).
   \]
4. Use
   \[
   \tan\theta=\frac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}
   \]
   to connect equations involving both sine and cosine.
5. Use
   \[
   \sin^2\theta+\cos^2\theta=1
   \]
   to simplify expressions, prove identities and rewrite equations.
6. Solve trig equations in a given interval, including \(\sin x=k\), \(\cos x=k\), \(\tan x=k\), equations involving \(2x\) or \(3x\), and quadratics such as
   \[
   5\sin^2x+3\sin x-2=0.
   \]

## 6. Big Picture Explanation

Trigonometry begins as triangle language:

\[
\sin\theta=\frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{hypotenuse}},\qquad
\cos\theta=\frac{\text{adjacent}}{\text{hypotenuse}},\qquad
\tan\theta=\frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{adjacent}}.
\]

But A-Level trig quickly becomes function language. The unit circle lets sine, cosine and tangent escape the small right-angled triangle and work for all angles. Once you know where trig functions are positive, negative, repeated and symmetric, you can solve equations with many possible answers rather than being trapped by the calculator's single principal value.

This chapter has three engine parts:

1. **Geometry engine**: exact values and unit circle meaning.
2. **Identity engine**: rewriting using \(\tan\theta=\frac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}\) and \(\sin^2\theta+\cos^2\theta=1\).
3. **Equation engine**: solving in the required interval, including transformed arguments and quadratics.

## 7. Key Definitions and Notation

### 7.1 Right-triangle definitions

For a right-angled triangle with angle \(\theta\):

\[
\sin\theta=\frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{hypotenuse}},
\]

\[
\cos\theta=\frac{\text{adjacent}}{\text{hypotenuse}},
\]

\[
\tan\theta=\frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{adjacent}}.
\]

### 7.2 Complementary angles

Two angles are **complementary** if they add to \(90^\circ\).

The co-function identity is:

\[
\sin\theta=\cos(90^\circ-\theta),
\]

\[
\cos\theta=\sin(90^\circ-\theta).
\]

Example:

\[
\sin 40^\circ=\cos 50^\circ
\]

because

\[
40^\circ+50^\circ=90^\circ.
\]

### 7.3 Unit circle notation

On the unit circle, the point making angle \(\theta\) with the positive \(x\)-axis has coordinates

\[
(\cos\theta,\sin\theta).
\]

That means:

\[
x\text{-coordinate}=\cos\theta,
\]

\[
y\text{-coordinate}=\sin\theta.
\]

Since gradient is

\[
\frac{\text{change in }y}{\text{change in }x},
\]

we get

\[
\tan\theta=\frac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}.
\]

### 7.4 Identity notation

The symbol \(\equiv\) means “identically equal to”. It means the left-hand side and right-hand side are equal for all values where both sides are defined.

For example:

\[
\sin^2\theta+\cos^2\theta\equiv 1.
\]

### 7.5 Squared trig notation

\[
\sin^2\theta
\]

means

\[
(\sin\theta)^2.
\]

It does **not** mean

\[
\sin(\theta^2).
\]

## 8. Core Theory

### 8.1 Exact values for \(30^\circ,45^\circ,60^\circ\)

#### Exact values from half a unit square

Draw a square of side length \(1\). Cut it along a diagonal.

You get a right-angled isosceles triangle with sides:

\[
1,\quad 1,\quad \sqrt{1^2+1^2}.
\]

By Pythagoras:

\[
\text{hypotenuse}=\sqrt{1^2+1^2}
\]

\[
=\sqrt{1+1}
\]

\[
=\sqrt2.
\]

The two acute angles are both \(45^\circ\). Therefore:

\[
\sin45^\circ=\frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{hypotenuse}}
\]

\[
=\frac{1}{\sqrt2}
\]

\[
=\frac{\sqrt2}{2}.
\]

Also:

\[
\cos45^\circ=\frac{\text{adjacent}}{\text{hypotenuse}}
\]

\[
=\frac{1}{\sqrt2}
\]

\[
=\frac{\sqrt2}{2}.
\]

And:

\[
\tan45^\circ=\frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{adjacent}}
\]

\[
=\frac11
\]

\[
=1.
\]

#### Exact values from half an equilateral triangle

Draw an equilateral triangle of side length \(2\). Split it in half.

The half-triangle has:

\[
\text{hypotenuse}=2,
\]

\[
\text{short side}=1,
\]

and the remaining side is found by Pythagoras:

\[
\sqrt{2^2-1^2}
\]

\[
=\sqrt{4-1}
\]

\[
=\sqrt3.
\]

The angles are \(30^\circ\), \(60^\circ\), \(90^\circ\).

For \(30^\circ\):

\[
\sin30^\circ=\frac{1}{2},
\]

\[
\cos30^\circ=\frac{\sqrt3}{2},
\]

\[
\tan30^\circ=\frac{1}{\sqrt3}=\frac{\sqrt3}{3}.
\]

For \(60^\circ\):

\[
\sin60^\circ=\frac{\sqrt3}{2},
\]

\[
\cos60^\circ=\frac{1}{2},
\]

\[
\tan60^\circ=\sqrt3.
\]

#### Exact value table

| \(\theta\) | \(\sin\theta\) | \(\cos\theta\) | \(\tan\theta\) |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| \(30^\circ\) | \(\frac12\) | \(\frac{\sqrt3}{2}\) | \(\frac1{\sqrt3}=\frac{\sqrt3}{3}\) |
| \(45^\circ\) | \(\frac1{\sqrt2}=\frac{\sqrt2}{2}\) | \(\frac1{\sqrt2}=\frac{\sqrt2}{2}\) | \(1\) |
| \(60^\circ\) | \(\frac{\sqrt3}{2}\) | \(\frac12\) | \(\sqrt3\) |

### 8.2 Unit circle signs and special angles

On the unit circle:

\[
(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)
\]

gives the point reached by rotating anticlockwise from the positive \(x\)-axis.

At the key axis angles:

| Angle | \(\cos\theta\) | \(\sin\theta\) | \(\tan\theta\) |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| \(0^\circ\) | \(1\) | \(0\) | \(0\) |
| \(90^\circ\) | \(0\) | \(1\) | undefined |
| \(180^\circ\) | \(-1\) | \(0\) | \(0\) |
| \(270^\circ\) | \(0\) | \(-1\) | undefined |
| \(360^\circ\) | \(1\) | \(0\) | \(0\) |

Why is \(\tan90^\circ\) undefined?

\[
\tan\theta=\frac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}.
\]

At \(90^\circ\):

\[
\sin90^\circ=1,
\]

\[
\cos90^\circ=0,
\]

so

\[
\tan90^\circ=\frac10,
\]

which is undefined.

#### Quadrant signs

| Range | \(\cos\theta\) | \(\sin\theta\) | \(\tan\theta\) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| \(0^\circ<\theta<90^\circ\) | \(+\) | \(+\) | \(+\) |
| \(90^\circ<\theta<180^\circ\) | \(-\) | \(+\) | \(-\) |
| \(180^\circ<\theta<270^\circ\) | \(-\) | \(-\) | \(+\) |
| \(270^\circ<\theta<360^\circ\) | \(+\) | \(-\) | \(-\) |

Reason:

\[
\tan\theta=\frac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}.
\]

So tangent is positive when sine and cosine have the same sign, and negative when they have different signs.

### 8.3 Angle laws from symmetry and periodicity

The evidence highlights these laws as fast alternatives to drawing the graph every time:

\[
\sin x=\sin(180^\circ-x),
\]

\[
\cos x=\cos(360^\circ-x),
\]

\[
\sin(x+360^\circ)=\sin x,
\]

\[
\cos(x+360^\circ)=\cos x,
\]

\[
\tan(x+180^\circ)=\tan x.
\]

Also:

\[
\sin x=\cos(90^\circ-x).
\]

These are memory tools, but they come from graphs and the unit circle.

### 8.4 Two core identities

#### Identity 1: Tangent identity

From a right triangle with hypotenuse \(1\):

\[
\sin\theta=\frac{\text{opposite}}{1}=\text{opposite},
\]

\[
\cos\theta=\frac{\text{adjacent}}{1}=\text{adjacent}.
\]

Now:

\[
\tan\theta=\frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{adjacent}}.
\]

Substitute:

\[
\tan\theta=\frac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}.
\]

So:

\[
\boxed{\tan\theta=\frac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}}.
\]

#### Identity 2: Pythagorean identity

Using the same triangle:

\[
\text{opposite}=\sin\theta,
\]

\[
\text{adjacent}=\cos\theta,
\]

\[
\text{hypotenuse}=1.
\]

Pythagoras gives:

\[
(\sin\theta)^2+(\cos\theta)^2=1^2.
\]

So:

\[
\sin^2\theta+\cos^2\theta=1.
\]

Hence:

\[
\boxed{\sin^2\theta+\cos^2\theta=1}.
\]

Rearranged forms:

\[
\sin^2\theta=1-\cos^2\theta,
\]

\[
\cos^2\theta=1-\sin^2\theta.
\]

### 8.5 Proof strategy for identities

To prove an identity:

1. Start with the messier side.
2. Convert \(\tan\) into \(\frac{\sin}{\cos}\) if useful.
3. Combine fractions into one fraction when adding or subtracting.
4. Look for:
   \[
   1-\sin^2\theta=\cos^2\theta
   \]
   or
   \[
   1-\cos^2\theta=\sin^2\theta.
   \]
5. Work step by step until you reach the other side.
6. End with a clear statement:
   \[
   =\text{RHS}.
   \]

### 8.6 Solving simple trig equations

A calculator gives one value when using:

\[
\sin^{-1},\qquad \cos^{-1},\qquad \tan^{-1}.
\]

This is the **principal value**. The exam work is finding every solution in the required interval.

#### For sine

Example:

\[
\sin\theta=\frac12,\qquad 0^\circ\leq\theta\leq360^\circ.
\]

First:

\[
\theta=\sin^{-1}\left(\frac12\right)
\]

\[
=30^\circ.
\]

Second sine solution:

\[
\theta=180^\circ-30^\circ
\]

\[
=150^\circ.
\]

So:

\[
\boxed{\theta=30^\circ,\ 150^\circ}.
\]

#### For cosine

Use:

\[
\cos x=\cos(360^\circ-x).
\]

Example:

\[
2\cos\theta=\sqrt3,
\qquad 0^\circ\leq\theta\leq360^\circ.
\]

Divide by \(2\):

\[
\cos\theta=\frac{\sqrt3}{2}.
\]

First solution:

\[
\theta=30^\circ.
\]

Second solution:

\[
\theta=360^\circ-30^\circ
\]

\[
=330^\circ.
\]

So:

\[
\boxed{\theta=30^\circ,\ 330^\circ}.
\]

#### For tangent

Tangent repeats every \(180^\circ\):

\[
\tan(x+180^\circ)=\tan x.
\]

Example:

\[
5\tan\theta=10,
\qquad -180^\circ\leq\theta<180^\circ.
\]

Divide by \(5\):

\[
\tan\theta=2.
\]

First solution:

\[
\theta=\tan^{-1}(2)
\]

\[
=63.4^\circ\quad \text{to 1 d.p.}
\]

Another tangent solution:

\[
\theta=63.4^\circ-180^\circ
\]

\[
=-116.6^\circ.
\]

Both lie in the interval, so:

\[
\boxed{\theta=63.4^\circ,\ -116.6^\circ}.
\]

### 8.7 Equations involving both sine and cosine

When an equation contains both \(\sin\theta\) and \(\cos\theta\), try dividing by \(\cos\theta\) to create tangent.

Example:

\[
\sin\theta=\sqrt3\cos\theta,
\qquad 0^\circ\leq\theta\leq360^\circ.
\]

Divide both sides by \(\cos\theta\):

\[
\frac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}=\sqrt3.
\]

Use:

\[
\frac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}=\tan\theta.
\]

So:

\[
\tan\theta=\sqrt3.
\]

First solution:

\[
\theta=\tan^{-1}(\sqrt3)
\]

\[
=60^\circ.
\]

Tangent repeats every \(180^\circ\), so:

\[
\theta=60^\circ+180^\circ
\]

\[
=240^\circ.
\]

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{\theta=60^\circ,\ 240^\circ}.
\]

### 8.8 Harder equations with linear arguments

The **argument** is the expression inside the trig function. For example, in \(\cos(3x)\), the argument is \(3x\).

#### Method

For equations such as

\[
\cos(3x)=-\frac12,\qquad 0^\circ\leq x\leq360^\circ,
\]

do not divide by \(3\) too early.

Step 1: Adjust the interval.

Since

\[
0^\circ\leq x\leq360^\circ,
\]

multiply everything by \(3\):

\[
0^\circ\leq 3x\leq1080^\circ.
\]

Step 2: Solve for \(3x\).

\[
3x=\cos^{-1}\left(-\frac12\right)
\]

\[
=120^\circ.
\]

The second cosine value in the first cycle is:

\[
3x=360^\circ-120^\circ
\]

\[
=240^\circ.
\]

Now keep adding \(360^\circ\) until the upper bound \(1080^\circ\) is reached:

\[
3x=120^\circ,\ 480^\circ,\ 840^\circ,
\]

\[
3x=240^\circ,\ 600^\circ,\ 960^\circ.
\]

Step 3: Divide every value by \(3\):

\[
x=40^\circ,\ 160^\circ,\ 280^\circ,
\]

\[
x=80^\circ,\ 200^\circ,\ 320^\circ.
\]

In ascending order:

\[
\boxed{x=40^\circ,\ 80^\circ,\ 160^\circ,\ 200^\circ,\ 280^\circ,\ 320^\circ}.
\]

### 8.9 Quadratics in \(\sin\), \(\cos\) and \(\tan\)

A trig equation can be quadratic in a trig function.

Example:

\[
5\sin^2x+3\sin x-2=0,
\qquad 0^\circ\leq x\leq360^\circ.
\]

You may set:

\[
y=\sin x.
\]

Then:

\[
y^2=\sin^2x.
\]

So:

\[
5y^2+3y-2=0.
\]

Factorise:

\[
(5y-2)(y+1)=0.
\]

So:

\[
5y-2=0
\]

or

\[
y+1=0.
\]

Hence:

\[
y=\frac25
\]

or

\[
y=-1.
\]

Substitute back:

\[
\sin x=\frac25
\]

or

\[
\sin x=-1.
\]

For \(\sin x=\frac25\):

\[
x=\sin^{-1}\left(\frac25\right)=23.6^\circ.
\]

Second sine solution:

\[
x=180^\circ-23.6^\circ=156.4^\circ.
\]

For \(\sin x=-1\):

\[
x=270^\circ.
\]

So:

\[
\boxed{x=23.6^\circ,\ 156.4^\circ,\ 270^\circ}.
\]

### 8.10 Mixed sine and cosine quadratics

Example:

\[
2\cos^2x+9\sin x=3\sin^2x,
\qquad -180^\circ\leq x\leq180^\circ.
\]

The equation contains:

\[
\cos^2x,\quad \sin x,\quad \sin^2x.
\]

Use:

\[
\cos^2x=1-\sin^2x.
\]

Substitute carefully, with brackets:

\[
2(1-\sin^2x)+9\sin x=3\sin^2x.
\]

Expand:

\[
2-2\sin^2x+9\sin x=3\sin^2x.
\]

Bring everything to one side:

\[
2-2\sin^2x+9\sin x-3\sin^2x=0.
\]

Combine like terms:

\[
2-5\sin^2x+9\sin x=0.
\]

Multiply by \(-1\):

\[
5\sin^2x-9\sin x-2=0.
\]

Factorise:

\[
(5\sin x+1)(\sin x-2)=0.
\]

So:

\[
5\sin x+1=0
\]

or

\[
\sin x-2=0.
\]

Therefore:

\[
\sin x=-\frac15
\]

or

\[
\sin x=2.
\]

But:

\[
-1\leq\sin x\leq 1.
\]

So \(\sin x=2\) has no real solutions.

Now solve:

\[
\sin x=-\frac15.
\]

Principal value:

\[
x=\sin^{-1}\left(-\frac15\right)=-11.5^\circ \quad \text{to 1 d.p.}
\]

Second sine value:

\[
x=180^\circ-(-11.5^\circ)=191.5^\circ.
\]

But \(191.5^\circ\) is outside \(-180^\circ\leq x\leq180^\circ\). Use periodicity:

\[
191.5^\circ-360^\circ=-168.5^\circ.
\]

So the solutions are:

\[
\boxed{x=-168.5^\circ,\ -11.5^\circ}.
\]

## 10. Worked Examples

### Worked Example 1: Evaluate without a calculator

Find:

\[
\tan225^\circ.
\]

Since tangent repeats every \(180^\circ\):

\[
\tan225^\circ=\tan(225^\circ-180^\circ).
\]

\[
=\tan45^\circ.
\]

\[
=1.
\]

So:

\[
\boxed{\tan225^\circ=1}.
\]

### Worked Example 2: Evaluate \(\sin150^\circ\)

Use:

\[
\sin x=\sin(180^\circ-x).
\]

\[
\sin150^\circ=\sin(180^\circ-150^\circ).
\]

\[
=\sin30^\circ.
\]

\[
=\frac12.
\]

So:

\[
\boxed{\sin150^\circ=\frac12}.
\]

### Worked Example 3: Prove an identity

Prove:

\[
1-\tan\theta\sin\theta\cos\theta\equiv \cos^2\theta.
\]

Start with the left-hand side:

\[
\text{LHS}=1-\tan\theta\sin\theta\cos\theta.
\]

Use:

\[
\tan\theta=\frac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}.
\]

So:

\[
\text{LHS}=1-\frac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}\sin\theta\cos\theta.
\]

Multiply the trig factors:

\[
\text{LHS}=1-\frac{\sin^2\theta\cos\theta}{\cos\theta}.
\]

Cancel \(\cos\theta\), where defined:

\[
\text{LHS}=1-\sin^2\theta.
\]

Use:

\[
1-\sin^2\theta=\cos^2\theta.
\]

Therefore:

\[
\text{LHS}=\cos^2\theta=\text{RHS}.
\]

Hence:

\[
\boxed{1-\tan\theta\sin\theta\cos\theta\equiv \cos^2\theta}.
\]

### Worked Example 4: Prove another identity

Prove:

\[
\tan\theta+\frac1{\tan\theta}\equiv \frac1{\sin\theta\cos\theta}.
\]

Start with the left-hand side:

\[
\text{LHS}=\tan\theta+\frac1{\tan\theta}.
\]

Use:

\[
\tan\theta=\frac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta},
\]

so:

\[
\frac1{\tan\theta}=\frac{\cos\theta}{\sin\theta}.
\]

Then:

\[
\text{LHS}=\frac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}+\frac{\cos\theta}{\sin\theta}.
\]

Use common denominator \(\sin\theta\cos\theta\):

\[
\text{LHS}=\frac{\sin^2\theta}{\sin\theta\cos\theta}+\frac{\cos^2\theta}{\sin\theta\cos\theta}.
\]

Combine:

\[
\text{LHS}=\frac{\sin^2\theta+\cos^2\theta}{\sin\theta\cos\theta}.
\]

Use:

\[
\sin^2\theta+\cos^2\theta=1.
\]

So:

\[
\text{LHS}=\frac1{\sin\theta\cos\theta}.
\]

Therefore:

\[
\text{LHS}=\text{RHS}.
\]

Hence:

\[
\boxed{\tan\theta+\frac1{\tan\theta}\equiv \frac1{\sin\theta\cos\theta}}.
\]

### Worked Example 5: Solve a simple equation

Solve:

\[
\sin\theta=-\frac12,
\qquad 0^\circ\leq\theta\leq360^\circ.
\]

Principal value:

\[
\theta=\sin^{-1}\left(-\frac12\right)
\]

\[
=-30^\circ.
\]

This is not in the interval. Use sine symmetry:

\[
\theta=180^\circ-(-30^\circ)
\]

\[
=210^\circ.
\]

Use periodicity:

\[
\theta=-30^\circ+360^\circ
\]

\[
=330^\circ.
\]

So:

\[
\boxed{\theta=210^\circ,\ 330^\circ}.
\]

### Worked Example 6: Solve a transformed argument equation

Solve:

\[
\sin(2x+30^\circ)=\frac1{\sqrt2},
\qquad 0^\circ\leq x\leq360^\circ.
\]

First transform the interval.

Since:

\[
0^\circ\leq x\leq360^\circ,
\]

multiply by \(2\):

\[
0^\circ\leq 2x\leq720^\circ.
\]

Add \(30^\circ\):

\[
30^\circ\leq 2x+30^\circ\leq750^\circ.
\]

Now solve:

\[
\sin(2x+30^\circ)=\frac1{\sqrt2}.
\]

Since:

\[
\sin45^\circ=\frac1{\sqrt2},
\]

the first solutions for the argument are:

\[
2x+30^\circ=45^\circ
\]

and

\[
2x+30^\circ=180^\circ-45^\circ=135^\circ.
\]

Add \(360^\circ\) where still inside the argument interval:

\[
2x+30^\circ=45^\circ,\ 135^\circ,\ 405^\circ,\ 495^\circ.
\]

Now subtract \(30^\circ\):

\[
2x=15^\circ,\ 105^\circ,\ 375^\circ,\ 465^\circ.
\]

Divide by \(2\):

\[
x=7.5^\circ,\ 52.5^\circ,\ 187.5^\circ,\ 232.5^\circ.
\]

So:

\[
\boxed{x=7.5^\circ,\ 52.5^\circ,\ 187.5^\circ,\ 232.5^\circ}.
\]

## 12. Common Mistakes and Exam Traps

### Trap 1: Using only the calculator value

A calculator gives only one principal value. For example:

\[
\sin^{-1}\left(\frac12\right)=30^\circ,
\]

but in

\[
0^\circ\leq\theta\leq360^\circ,
\]

the equation

\[
\sin\theta=\frac12
\]

has two solutions:

\[
30^\circ,\quad 150^\circ.
\]

### Trap 2: Dividing by \(3\) too early in \(\cos(3x)\)

For

\[
\cos(3x)=-\frac12,
\]

first solve for \(3x\) across the adjusted interval. Only divide by \(3\) at the very end.

### Trap 3: Forgetting the \(\pm\) after square rooting

For:

\[
\tan^2\theta=4,
\]

you must write:

\[
\tan\theta=2
\]

or

\[
\tan\theta=-2.
\]

Missing the negative case loses multiple solutions.

### Trap 4: Forgetting brackets when substituting an identity

If:

\[
\cos^2x=1-\sin^2x,
\]

then:

\[
2\cos^2x=2(1-\sin^2x),
\]

not:

\[
2-\sin^2x.
\]

### Trap 5: Accepting impossible trig values

Since:

\[
-1\leq\sin x\leq1,
\]

the equation \(\sin x=2\) has no real solutions.

### Trap 6: Treating graphical or numerical solving as full method

The transcript evidence warns that answers based entirely on graphical or numerical methods may not be acceptable for exam questions. Use graphing tools to check, not to replace algebraic working.

## 13. Exam Technique

1. **Write the interval first.** It controls the number of answers.
2. **Use degrees unless told otherwise.** This chapter evidence is in degrees.
3. **For identities, start with the messier side.** Turn tangent into sine and cosine if it helps.
4. **For linear arguments, transform the interval.** For example:
   \[
   0^\circ\leq x\leq180^\circ
   \]
   becomes:
   \[
   -10^\circ\leq 3x-10^\circ\leq530^\circ
   \]
   for an argument of \(3x-10^\circ\).
5. **For quadratic trig equations, solve the quadratic first, then solve the trig equations.**
6. **Check impossible values.** If you get \(\sin x=2\), stop that branch.
7. **Keep extra decimals until the final answer.** Rounding early can change a final answer, especially after adding, subtracting and dividing.

### Solution 2

\[
\tan(-45^\circ)=-\tan45^\circ.
\]

\[
=-1.
\]

\[

\]

### Solution 3

Prove:

\[
\tan^2\theta\equiv \frac1{\cos^2\theta}-1.
\]

Start with the left-hand side:

\[
\text{LHS}=\tan^2\theta.
\]

Use:

\[
\tan\theta=\frac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}.
\]

So:

\[
\text{LHS}=\left(\frac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}\right)^2.
\]

\[
=\frac{\sin^2\theta}{\cos^2\theta}.
\]

Use:

\[
\sin^2\theta=1-\cos^2\theta.
\]

Then:

\[
\text{LHS}=\frac{1-\cos^2\theta}{\cos^2\theta}.
\]

Split the fraction:

\[
\text{LHS}=\frac1{\cos^2\theta}-\frac{\cos^2\theta}{\cos^2\theta}.
\]

\[
=\frac1{\cos^2\theta}-1.
\]

\[
=\text{RHS}.
\]

Hence:

\[
\boxed{\tan^2\theta\equiv \frac1{\cos^2\theta}-1}.
\]

### Solution 4

Solve:

\[
3\sin\theta=\cos\theta,
\qquad -180^\circ\leq\theta\leq180^\circ.
\]

Divide both sides by \(\cos\theta\):

\[
\frac{3\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}=\frac{\cos\theta}{\cos\theta}.
\]

\[
3\frac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}=1.
\]

Use:

\[
\frac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}=\tan\theta.
\]

So:

\[
3\tan\theta=1.
\]

Divide by \(3\):

\[
\tan\theta=\frac13.
\]

Principal value:

\[
\theta=\tan^{-1}\left(\frac13\right)=18.4^\circ \quad \text{to 1 d.p.}
\]

Tangent repeats every \(180^\circ\):

\[
\theta=18.4^\circ-180^\circ=-161.6^\circ.
\]

So:

\[
\boxed{\theta=-161.6^\circ,\ 18.4^\circ}.
\]

### Solution 5

Solve:

\[
\tan^2\theta=4,
\qquad 0^\circ\leq\theta\leq360^\circ.
\]

Square root both sides:

\[
\tan\theta=\pm2.
\]

So:

\[
\tan\theta=2
\]

or

\[
\tan\theta=-2.
\]

For \(\tan\theta=2\):

\[
\theta=\tan^{-1}(2)=63.4^\circ.
\]

Add \(180^\circ\):

\[
\theta=63.4^\circ+180^\circ=243.4^\circ.
\]

For \(\tan\theta=-2\):

\[
\theta=\tan^{-1}(-2)=-63.4^\circ.
\]

This is outside the interval, so add \(180^\circ\):

\[
-63.4^\circ+180^\circ=116.6^\circ.
\]

Add another \(180^\circ\):

\[
116.6^\circ+180^\circ=296.6^\circ.
\]

So:

\[
\boxed{\theta=63.4^\circ,\ 116.6^\circ,\ 243.4^\circ,\ 296.6^\circ}.
\]

### Solution 6

Show that:

\[
5\sin x=1+2\cos^2x
\]

can be written as:

\[
2\sin^2x+5\sin x-3=0.
\]

Use:

\[
\cos^2x=1-\sin^2x.
\]

Substitute:

\[
5\sin x=1+2(1-\sin^2x).
\]

Expand:

\[
5\sin x=1+2-2\sin^2x.
\]

\[
5\sin x=3-2\sin^2x.
\]

Bring everything to one side:

\[
2\sin^2x+5\sin x-3=0.
\]

Hence shown.

\[
\boxed{2\sin^2x+5\sin x-3=0}.
\]

### Solution 7

Solve:

\[
5\sin x=1+2\cos^2x,
\qquad 0^\circ\leq x\leq360^\circ.
\]

From Solution 6:

\[
2\sin^2x+5\sin x-3=0.
\]

Factorise:

\[
(2\sin x-1)(\sin x+3)=0.
\]

So:

\[
2\sin x-1=0
\]

or

\[
\sin x+3=0.
\]

Hence:

\[
\sin x=\frac12
\]

or

\[
\sin x=-3.
\]

But \(\sin x=-3\) has no solutions because:

\[
-1\leq\sin x\leq1.
\]

Now solve:

\[
\sin x=\frac12.
\]

First solution:

\[
x=30^\circ.
\]

Second sine solution:

\[
x=180^\circ-30^\circ=150^\circ.
\]

So:

\[
\boxed{x=30^\circ,\ 150^\circ}.
\]
