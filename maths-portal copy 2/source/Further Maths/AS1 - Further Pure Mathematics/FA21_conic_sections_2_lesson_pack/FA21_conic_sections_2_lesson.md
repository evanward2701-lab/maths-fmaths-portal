# 6. Big Picture Explanation

Conics are curves obtained when a plane intersects a non-solid double cone. A circle occurs when the plane is parallel to the base; an ellipse occurs when the plane is less steep than the cone surface; a parabola occurs when the plane is parallel to the cone surface; and a hyperbola occurs when the plane is steeper than the cone surface and intersects both cones.

The core bridge is:

\[
x^2+y^2=1,\qquad x=\cos\theta,\qquad y=\sin\theta.
\]

Stretching gives:

\[
x=a\cos\theta,\qquad y=b\sin\theta,
\]

and therefore:

\[
\frac{x^2}{a^2}+\frac{y^2}{b^2}=1.
\]

The hyperbola changes the plus sign to a minus sign:

\[
\frac{x^2}{a^2}-\frac{y^2}{b^2}=1.
\]

That sign change creates branches, asymptotes and a different eccentricity relation.

# 8. Core Theory for the Enrichment Lesson

## 8.1 Conics from a Double Cone

A conic section is formed when a plane intersects a non-solid double cone. The conic is the line of intersection, not the shaded region.

## 8.2 Deriving the Standard Ellipse from the Unit Circle

Start with:

\[
x^2+y^2=1.
\]

Use:

\[
x=\cos\theta,\qquad y=\sin\theta.
\]

Stretch horizontally by \(a\) and vertically by \(b\):

\[
x=a\cos\theta,\qquad y=b\sin\theta.
\]

Then:

\[
\frac xa=\cos\theta,\qquad \frac yb=\sin\theta.
\]

Square and add:

\[
\frac{x^2}{a^2}+\frac{y^2}{b^2}=\cos^2\theta+\sin^2\theta=1.
\]

So:

\[
\boxed{\frac{x^2}{a^2}+\frac{y^2}{b^2}=1.}
\]

## 8.3 Reading an Ellipse

For:

\[
\frac{x^2}{a^2}+\frac{y^2}{b^2}=1,
\]

setting \(y=0\) gives \(x=\pm a\), and setting \(x=0\) gives \(y=\pm b\). If \(a>b\), the major axis is horizontal. If \(b>a\), the major axis is vertical.

## 8.4 Worked Example: \(4x^2+9y^2=36\)

Divide by \(36\):

\[
\frac{4x^2}{36}+\frac{9y^2}{36}=1.
\]

So:

\[
\frac{x^2}{9}+\frac{y^2}{4}=1.
\]

Thus:

\[
a=3,\qquad b=2.
\]

The sketch has intercepts:

\[
(-3,0),(3,0),(0,-2),(0,2).
\]

Parametric equations:

\[
\boxed{x=3\cos\theta,\qquad y=2\sin\theta.}
\]

## 8.5 Parametric Ellipse to Cartesian Form

If:

\[
x=3\cos\theta,\qquad y=5\sin\theta,
\]

then:

\[
\frac x3=\cos\theta,\qquad \frac y5=\sin\theta.
\]

So:

\[
\frac{x^2}{9}+\frac{y^2}{25}=\cos^2\theta+\sin^2\theta=1.
\]

Hence:

\[
\boxed{\frac{x^2}{9}+\frac{y^2}{25}=1.}
\]

## 8.6 Standard Hyperbola

\[
\boxed{\frac{x^2}{a^2}-\frac{y^2}{b^2}=1}
\]

Set \(y=0\):

\[
\frac{x^2}{a^2}=1 \Rightarrow x=\pm a.
\]

Set \(x=0\):

\[
-\frac{y^2}{b^2}=1,
\]

which gives no real \(y\)-intercepts.

As \(|x|\) becomes large:

\[
\frac{x^2}{a^2}\approx \frac{y^2}{b^2},
\]

so:

\[
\boxed{y=\pm\frac ba x.}
\]

## 8.7 Hyperbola Parametric Forms

Using:

\[
x=a\cosh t,\qquad y=b\sinh t,
\]

we get:

\[
\frac{x^2}{a^2}-\frac{y^2}{b^2}=\cosh^2t-\sinh^2t=1.
\]

Using:

\[
x=a\sec\theta,\qquad y=b\tan\theta,
\]

we get:

\[
\frac{x^2}{a^2}-\frac{y^2}{b^2}=\sec^2\theta-\tan^2\theta=1.
\]

## 8.8 Worked Example: \(9x^2-4y^2=36\)

Divide by \(36\):

\[
\frac{9x^2}{36}-\frac{4y^2}{36}=1.
\]

So:

\[
\frac{x^2}{4}-\frac{y^2}{9}=1.
\]

Thus:

\[
a=2,\qquad b=3.
\]

Vertices:

\[
(-2,0),(2,0).
\]

Asymptotes:

\[
\boxed{y=\pm\frac32x.}
\]

Parametric equations:

\[
\boxed{x=2\sec\theta,\qquad y=3\tan\theta}
\]

or:

\[
\boxed{x=\pm2\cosh t,\qquad y=3\sinh t.}
\]

## 8.9 Eccentricity, Foci and Directrices

Let \(P\) be a point on the conic, \(S\) a focus and \(PD\) the perpendicular distance to a directrix.

\[
e=\frac{PS}{PD}.
\]

So:

\[
ePD=PS.
\]

For a horizontal ellipse:

\[
\frac{x^2}{a^2}+\frac{y^2}{b^2}=1,\qquad a>b,
\]

\[
b^2=a^2(1-e^2),\qquad \text{foci }(\pm ae,0),\qquad \text{directrices }x=\pm\frac ae.
\]

For a vertical ellipse:

\[
a^2=b^2(1-e^2),\qquad \text{foci }(0,\pm be),\qquad \text{directrices }y=\pm\frac be.
\]

For a standard horizontal hyperbola:

\[
b^2=a^2(e^2-1),\qquad \text{foci }(\pm ae,0),\qquad \text{directrices }x=\pm\frac ae.
\]

## 8.10 Deriving the Ellipse Relation

Take focus \(S=(ae,0)\), directrix \(x=\frac ae\), and \(P=(x,y)\). Then:

\[
PD=\frac ae-x,
\]

and:

\[
PS^2=(x-ae)^2+y^2.
\]

Using \(e^2PD^2=PS^2\):

\[
e^2\left(\frac ae-x\right)^2=(x-ae)^2+y^2.
\]

Expand:

\[
a^2-2aex+e^2x^2=x^2-2aex+a^2e^2+y^2.
\]

Cancel \(-2aex\):

\[
a^2+e^2x^2=x^2+a^2e^2+y^2.
\]

Rearrange:

\[
a^2(1-e^2)=x^2(1-e^2)+y^2.
\]

Divide by \(a^2(1-e^2)\):

\[
1=\frac{x^2}{a^2}+\frac{y^2}{a^2(1-e^2)}.
\]

So:

\[
\boxed{b^2=a^2(1-e^2).}
\]

## 8.11 Worked Example: Horizontal Ellipse Foci and Directrices

For:

\[
\frac{x^2}{9}+\frac{y^2}{4}=1,
\]

we have:

\[
a=3,\qquad b=2.
\]

Use:

\[
b^2=a^2(1-e^2).
\]

\[
4=9(1-e^2).
\]

\[
e^2=1-\frac49=\frac59.
\]

\[
e=\frac{\sqrt5}{3}.
\]

Foci:

\[
(\pm ae,0)=\left(\pm3\cdot\frac{\sqrt5}{3},0\right)=(\pm\sqrt5,0).
\]

Directrices:

\[
x=\pm\frac ae=\pm\frac3{\sqrt5/3}=\pm\frac9{\sqrt5}.
\]

## 8.12 Worked Example: Vertical Ellipse Foci and Directrices

For:

\[
\frac{x^2}{16}+\frac{y^2}{25}=1,
\]

we have:

\[
a=4,\qquad b=5.
\]

Since \(b>a\), use:

\[
a^2=b^2(1-e^2).
\]

\[
16=25(1-e^2).
\]

\[
e^2=1-\frac{16}{25}=\frac9{25},\qquad e=\frac35.
\]

Foci:

\[
(0,\pm be)=(0,\pm 5\cdot\frac35)=(0,\pm3).
\]

Directrices:

\[
y=\pm\frac be=\pm\frac5{3/5}=\pm\frac{25}3.
\]

## 8.13 Reverse Example from Directrix and Focus

If directrix \(x=8\) and corresponding focus \((2,0)\), then:

\[
\frac ae=8,\qquad ae=2.
\]

Multiply:

\[
a^2=16,\qquad a=4.
\]

Then:

\[
4e=2,\qquad e=\frac12.
\]

Use:

\[
b^2=a^2(1-e^2)=16\left(1-\frac14\right)=12.
\]

So:

\[
\boxed{a=4,\qquad b=2\sqrt3.}
\]

## 8.14 Ellipse Property \(PS+PS'=2a\)

For a horizontal ellipse, with foci \(S,S'\), any point \(P\) on the ellipse satisfies:

\[
\boxed{PS+PS'=2a.}
\]

Proof idea:

\[
PS=ePD,\qquad PS'=ePD'.
\]

So:

\[
PS+PS'=e(PD+PD').
\]

The directrices are \(x=\pm\frac ae\), so:

\[
PD+PD'=\frac{2a}e.
\]

Therefore:

\[
PS+PS'=e\cdot\frac{2a}e=2a.
\]

## 8.15 Deriving the Hyperbola Relation

Take:

\[
S=(ae,0),\qquad x=\frac ae,\qquad e>1.
\]

For a point \(P=(x,y)\) on the right-hand branch:

\[
PD=x-\frac ae,
\]

\[
PS^2=(x-ae)^2+y^2.
\]

Using \(e^2PD^2=PS^2\):

\[
e^2\left(x-\frac ae\right)^2=(x-ae)^2+y^2.
\]

Expand:

\[
e^2x^2-2aex+a^2=x^2-2aex+a^2e^2+y^2.
\]

Cancel:

\[
e^2x^2+a^2=x^2+a^2e^2+y^2.
\]

Rearrange:

\[
x^2(e^2-1)-y^2=a^2(e^2-1).
\]

Divide by \(a^2(e^2-1)\):

\[
\frac{x^2}{a^2}-\frac{y^2}{a^2(e^2-1)}=1.
\]

So:

\[
\boxed{b^2=a^2(e^2-1).}
\]

## 8.16 Worked Example: Hyperbola Foci and Asymptotes

For:

\[
\frac{x^2}{9}-\frac{y^2}{4}=1,
\]

\[
a=3,\qquad b=2.
\]

Use:

\[
4=9(e^2-1).
\]

\[
e^2=1+\frac49=\frac{13}9.
\]

\[
e=\frac{\sqrt{13}}3.
\]

Foci:

\[
(\pm ae,0)=(\pm\sqrt{13},0).
\]

Directrices:

\[
x=\pm\frac ae=\pm\frac9{\sqrt{13}}.
\]

Asymptotes:

\[
\boxed{y=\pm\frac23x.}
\]

## 8.17 Rectangular Hyperbola Side Note

The reciprocal-style rectangular hyperbola such as \(y=\frac1x\) is not the same as the standard horizontal hyperbola

\[
\frac{x^2}{a^2}-\frac{y^2}{b^2}=1.
\]

This side note is optional curiosity only and is excluded from the core enrichment sequence.

## 8.18 Tangents and Normals of Ellipses

For:

\[
x=a\cos\theta,\qquad y=b\sin\theta,
\]

\[
\frac{dx}{d\theta}=-a\sin\theta,\qquad \frac{dy}{d\theta}=b\cos\theta.
\]

So:

\[
\frac{dy}{dx}=\frac{b\cos\theta}{-a\sin\theta}.
\]

For the ellipse \(\frac{x^2}9+\frac{y^2}4=1\), at \((3\cos\theta,2\sin\theta)\),

\[
\frac{dy}{dx}=\frac{2\cos\theta}{-3\sin\theta}.
\]

Using point-gradient form:

\[
y-2\sin\theta=\frac{2\cos\theta}{-3\sin\theta}(x-3\cos\theta).
\]

This rearranges to:

\[
\boxed{2x\cos\theta+3y\sin\theta=6.}
\]

For a general ellipse, the normal at \(P(a\cos\theta,b\sin\theta)\) has gradient:

\[
m_N=\frac{a\sin\theta}{b\cos\theta}.
\]

The normal equation simplifies to:

\[
\boxed{by\cos\theta=ax\sin\theta+(b^2-a^2)\cos\theta\sin\theta.}
\]

## 8.19 Choosing the Correct Parameter

If \(x=4\cos\theta\), \(y=3\sin\theta\), and:

\[
P=\left(2,\frac{3\sqrt3}2\right),
\]

then \(2=4\cos\theta\) gives \(\cos\theta=\frac12\), but this alone gives more than one possible \(\theta\). Use \(y\) as well:

\[
\frac{3\sqrt3}2=3\sin\theta \Rightarrow \sin\theta=\frac{\sqrt3}2.
\]

The common value is:

\[
\theta=\frac\pi3.
\]

## 8.20 Tangent Condition for an Ellipse

Substitute \(y=mx+c\) into:

\[
\frac{x^2}{a^2}+\frac{y^2}{b^2}=1.
\]

After clearing denominators:

\[
(b^2+a^2m^2)x^2+2a^2mcx+a^2(c^2-b^2)=0.
\]

Tangency means discriminant \(=0\). This simplifies to:

\[
\boxed{a^2m^2+b^2=c^2.}
\]

## 8.21 Tangents and Normals of Hyperbolas

For a Cartesian point on:

\[
\frac{x^2}9-\frac{y^2}4=1,
\]

implicit differentiation gives:

\[
\frac{2x}9-\frac y2\frac{dy}{dx}=0,
\]

so:

\[
\frac{dy}{dx}=\frac{4x}{9y}.
\]

At \((6,2\sqrt3)\):

\[
m=\frac{4(6)}{9(2\sqrt3)}=\frac{4\sqrt3}9.
\]

So the tangent is:

\[
\boxed{y-2\sqrt3=\frac{4\sqrt3}9(x-6).}
\]

## 8.22 General Hyperbola Tangent

For:

\[
x=a\cosh t,\qquad y=b\sinh t,
\]

\[
\frac{dx}{dt}=a\sinh t,\qquad \frac{dy}{dt}=b\cosh t.
\]

So:

\[
\frac{dy}{dx}=\frac{b\cosh t}{a\sinh t}.
\]

Using point-gradient form at \(P(a\cosh t,b\sinh t)\):

\[
y-b\sinh t=\frac{b\cosh t}{a\sinh t}(x-a\cosh t).
\]

Multiply through:

\[
ay\sinh t-ab\sinh^2t=bx\cosh t-ab\cosh^2t.
\]

Use:

\[
\cosh^2t-\sinh^2t=1.
\]

Then:

\[
\boxed{bx\cosh t-ay\sinh t=ab.}
\]

## 8.23 Solving for \(t\) from a Tangent Intercept

For:

\[
\frac{x^2}9-\frac{y^2}4=1,
\]

the tangent at \((3\cosh t,2\sinh t)\) is:

\[
2x\cosh t-3y\sinh t=6.
\]

If it crosses the \(y\)-axis at \((0,-1)\), substitute:

\[
2(0)\cosh t-3(-1)\sinh t=6.
\]

So:

\[
3\sinh t=6,\qquad \sinh t=2.
\]

Thus:

\[
t=\operatorname{arsinh}(2)=\ln(2+\sqrt5).
\]

## 8.24 Tangent Condition for a Hyperbola

Substitute \(y=mx+c\) into:

\[
\frac{x^2}{a^2}-\frac{y^2}{b^2}=1.
\]

After clearing denominators:

\[
(b^2-a^2m^2)x^2-2a^2mcx-a^2(c^2+b^2)=0.
\]

Tangency means discriminant \(=0\). This simplifies to:

\[
\boxed{a^2m^2-b^2=c^2.}
\]

## 8.25 Loci Strategy

For locus questions: identify the moving point, construct the required point, call the locus point \(M=(X,Y)\), express \(X,Y\) in terms of the parameter, and eliminate using the correct identity.

## 8.26 Locus of Midpoint of Tangent Intercepts for an Ellipse

Tangent to:

\[
\frac{x^2}{a^2}+\frac{y^2}{b^2}=1
\]

at:

\[
(a\cos t,b\sin t)
\]

is:

\[
\frac{x\cos t}a+\frac{y\sin t}b=1.
\]

The intercepts are:

\[
A=(a\sec t,0),\qquad B=(0,b\cosec t).
\]

Midpoint:

\[
M=(X,Y)=\left(\frac a2\sec t,\frac b2\cosec t\right).
\]

So:

\[
\cos t=\frac a{2X},\qquad \sin t=\frac b{2Y}.
\]

Use \(\cos^2t+\sin^2t=1\):

\[
\boxed{\frac{a^2}{4X^2}+\frac{b^2}{4Y^2}=1.}
\]

## 8.27 Hyperbola Tangent Intercepts, Area and Midpoint

For:

\[
\frac{x^2}{A^2}-\frac{y^2}{B^2}=1,
\]

at:

\[
P=(A\cosh\theta,B\sinh\theta),
\]

the tangent is:

\[
Bx\cosh\theta-Ay\sinh\theta=AB.
\]

The intercepts are:

\[
Q=\left(\frac A{\cosh\theta},0\right),\qquad R=\left(0,-\frac B{\sinh\theta}\right).
\]

Area of \(\triangle OQR\):

\[
\frac12\cdot\frac A{\cosh\theta}\cdot\frac B{\sinh\theta}=\frac{AB}{2\sinh\theta\cosh\theta}=AB\operatorname{cosech}2\theta.
\]

Midpoint:

\[
M=(X,Y)=\left(\frac A{2\cosh\theta},-\frac B{2\sinh\theta}\right).
\]

So:

\[
\frac{A^2}{4X^2}-\frac{B^2}{4Y^2}=1.
\]

Rearrange:

\[
\boxed{Y^2=\frac{B^2X^2}{A^2-4X^2}.}
\]

# 12. Common Mistakes and Exam Traps

| Trap | What goes wrong | Repair |
|---|---|---|
| Treating this as confirmed CCEA core | The supplied CCEA map does not include conics. | Keep the off-spec enrichment label. |
| Not dividing through to RHS \(1\) | Wrong \(a,b\) values. | Standardise first. |
| Confusing \(a\) and \(a^2\) | Reads denominator as \(a\). | \(a\) is the square root of the denominator. |
| Using ellipse formula for vertical ellipse | Foci/directrices placed on wrong axis. | If \(b>a\), use \(a^2=b^2(1-e^2)\). |
| Using ellipse eccentricity formula for hyperbola | Gives impossible or wrong \(e\). | Use \(b^2=a^2(e^2-1)\). |
| Forgetting hyperbola asymptotes | Sketch lacks structure. | Always draw \(y=\pm\frac ba x\). |
| Differentiating \(\cosh t\) like \(\cos t\) | Wrong sign. | \(\frac d{dt}\cosh t=\sinh t\). |
| Choosing parameter from one coordinate only | Wrong point on curve. | Check both \(x\) and \(y\). |
| Reusing \(x,y\) for a locus point | Algebra tangles. | Use \(M=(X,Y)\). |
| Losing branch restrictions after squaring | Locus may show too much. | Keep original parameter/domain information. |

# 14. Worked Solutions

1. \(\frac{x^2}{16}+\frac{y^2}{9}=1\) is an ellipse with \(a=4,b=3\). \(\frac{x^2}{16}-\frac{y^2}{9}=1\) is a horizontal hyperbola with \(a=4,b=3\). \(x^2+y^2=25\) is a circle of radius \(5\). \(\frac{y^2}{9}-\frac{x^2}{4}=1\) is a vertical hyperbola.

2. \(25x^2+9y^2=225\):
\[
\frac{x^2}{9}+\frac{y^2}{25}=1,
\quad a=3,
\quad b=5,
\quad x=3\cos\theta,
\quad y=5\sin\theta.
\]

3. \(x=6\cos\theta,
 y=2\sin\theta\):
\[
\frac{x^2}{36}+\frac{y^2}{4}=1.
\]

4. \(16x^2-9y^2=144\):
\[
\frac{x^2}{9}-\frac{y^2}{16}=1,
\quad a=3,
\quad b=4,
\quad y=\pm\frac43x.
\]

5. From \(x=4\cos\theta\), \(y=7\sin\theta\):
\[
\frac x4=\cos\theta,
\quad \frac y7=\sin\theta,
\quad \frac{x^2}{16}+\frac{y^2}{49}=1.
\]

6. From \(x=5\sec\theta\), \(y=2\tan\theta\):
\[
\frac{x^2}{25}-\frac{y^2}{4}=\sec^2\theta-\tan^2\theta=1.
\]

7. For \(x=5\cos\theta,
 y=3\sin\theta\):
\[
\frac{dy}{dx}=\frac{3\cos\theta}{-5\sin\theta}.
\]
At \(\theta=\frac\pi6\):
\[
\frac{dy}{dx}=-\frac{3\sqrt3}{5}.
\]

8. For \(\frac{x^2}{4}-\frac{y^2}{25}=1\):
\[
\frac{x}{2}-\frac{2y}{25}\frac{dy}{dx}=0,
\quad \frac{dy}{dx}=\frac{25x}{4y}.
\]
At \((4,5\sqrt3)\):
\[
\frac{dy}{dx}=\frac{5\sqrt3}{3}.
\]

9. \(\frac{x^2}{25}+\frac{y^2}{9}=1\):
\[
e=\frac45,
\quad \text{foci }(\pm4,0),
\quad \text{directrices }x=\pm\frac{25}{4}.
\]

10. \(\frac{x^2}{9}+\frac{y^2}{49}=1\):
\[
e=\frac{2\sqrt{10}}7,
\quad \text{foci }(0,\pm2\sqrt{10}),
\quad \text{directrices }y=\pm\frac{49}{2\sqrt{10}}.
\]

11. With \(x=18\) and focus \((2,0)\):
\[
\frac ae=18,
\quad ae=2,
\quad a=6,
\quad e=\frac13,
\quad b=4\sqrt2.
\]

12. \(\frac{x^2}{16}-\frac{y^2}{9}=1\):
\[
e=\frac54,
\quad \text{foci }(\pm5,0),
\quad \text{directrices }x=\pm\frac{16}{5},
\quad \text{asymptotes }y=\pm\frac34x.
\]

13. At \(\theta=\frac\pi3\):
\[
P=\left(\frac52,\frac{3\sqrt3}{2}\right),
\quad m=-\frac{\sqrt3}{5}.
\]
Tangent:
\[
y-\frac{3\sqrt3}{2}=-\frac{\sqrt3}{5}\left(x-\frac52\right),
\quad \sqrt3x+5y=10\sqrt3.
\]

14. At \((4,5\sqrt3)\):
\[
m=\frac{25x}{4y}=\frac{5\sqrt3}{3}.
\]
Tangent:
\[
y-5\sqrt3=\frac{5\sqrt3}{3}(x-4).
\]

15. \(\frac{x^2}{36}+\frac{y^2}{20}=1\):
\[
a=6,
\quad b=2\sqrt5,
\quad e=\frac23,
\quad \text{foci }(\pm4,0),
\quad PS+PS'=12.
\]

16. The tangent to \(\frac{x^2}{16}+\frac{y^2}{9}=1\) at \((4\cos t,3\sin t)\) is:
\[
3x\cos t+4y\sin t=12.
\]
Intercepts:
\[
A=(4\sec t,0),
\quad B=(0,3\cosec t).
\]
Midpoint:
\[
M=(X,Y)=\left(2\sec t,\frac32\cosec t\right).
\]
Thus:
\[
\frac4{X^2}+\frac9{4Y^2}=1.
\]

17. Tangent:
\[
2x\cosh\theta-3y\sinh\theta=6.
\]
Intercepts:
\[
Q=\left(\frac3{\cosh\theta},0\right),
\quad R=\left(0,-\frac2{\sinh\theta}\right).
\]
Area:
\[
\frac12\cdot\frac3{\cosh\theta}\cdot\frac2{\sinh\theta}=6\operatorname{cosech}2\theta.
\]
Midpoint:
\[
M=\left(\frac3{2\cosh\theta},-\frac1{\sinh\theta}\right).
\]

# 15. Exam Technique Notes

1. Standardise first: the right-hand side must be \(1\).
2. Read \(a\) and \(b\) as square roots of denominators.
3. Ellipse: plus sign, closed curve.
4. Hyperbola: minus sign, branches and asymptotes.
5. For horizontal ellipses use \(b^2=a^2(1-e^2)\).
6. For vertical ellipses switch to \(a^2=b^2(1-e^2)\).
7. For standard horizontal hyperbolas use \(b^2=a^2(e^2-1)\).
8. Use exact surds and fractions.
9. Use parametric differentiation for parametric points.
10. Use implicit differentiation for Cartesian points.
11. Use discriminant \(=0\) for tangent conditions.
12. Use capital \(X,Y\) for locus points.
